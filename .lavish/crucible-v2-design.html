The highway model (your round-19 note): tracks are numbered lanes — Track 1, 2, 3… (wire format track-<n>) — on a highway where CRs are the vehicles. The mainline allocates CRs to lanes from their depends-on graph: independent CRs ride parallel lanes; dependent CRs queue in sequence. Track orchestrators have their own specific roles — enforcing the workflow rules and verifying that agents deliver their work accurately — the lane marshals of the highway. Wave boundary (round 20 — your semantics, my recommendation applied): the wave is where all lanes pause on completing their queues; the next wave launches after design reviews. Zero new API — wave state derives purely from plan states. The role hierarchy (your round-27 note): roles nest by scope — MAINLINE ORCHESTRATOR (widest: the whole project workflow — allocates lanes, launches waves, gates boundaries) → ORCHESTRATOR (track scope: one lane's CR queue — enforces workflow rules, verifies agent delivery) → RED / GREEN / VERIFY / FIX (narrowest: agentic roles scoped to one phase of one cycle). Every level is an AGENT to Crucible; the role determines scope and authority — who may file plans, confirm cycles, close CRs, and gate waves — while the tool itself stays outside the hierarchy (round 25).
